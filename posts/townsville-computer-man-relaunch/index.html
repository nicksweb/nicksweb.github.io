Townsville Computer Man is back — and yes, I started this in Year 12 with Notepad

Posted Jul 30, 2026 Updated Aug 24, 2026
By Nicholas “Nick” O'Sullivan, Townsville 4 min read

Townsville Computer Man is back.

Not “back” in the sense of a brand-new idea somebody dreamed up over a long weekend. Back in the sense of a name that has quietly outlasted MySpace, three different laptops of mine, and at least one genuinely cursed EFTPOS terminal. The relaunch is repair-focused — www.tsvcomputerman.com.au — but I figured the story behind it deserved more than a “we’re back!” banner and a stock photo of a screwdriver.

So here it is, warts and all.

It started with Notepad, not FrontPage

Long before there was a business, there was a Year 8 IT class, a computer lab, and me quietly refusing to touch Microsoft FrontPage. Everyone else was dragging and dropping. I was in Notepad, writing HTML by hand, because I’d decided — with all the confidence a thirteen-year-old can muster — that this was the “proper” way to do it. I ended up more or less running that corner of the class. Nobody assigned me that job. I just took it.

That stubborn streak turned out to be useful, because it’s basically the same instinct that runs the business today.

2008/2009: a domain, a bad host, and a big idea

The actual idea landed in 2008 heading into 2009. I wanted to fiddle with SEO and websites — properly fiddle, not just “make a site” — and my first domain for it was repair.nicksweb-design.net. It was hosted across a rotating cast of free and budget hosts including Awardspace and iPower, which tells you everything you need to know about the budget of a teenager running a computer repair side project. Remarkably, traces of that domain are still findable on the web in places today, like a fossil record of my earliest attempts at this whole thing.

I officially registered the business in my final year of Year 12 in 2009. Most of my classmates were worrying about exams. I was worrying about whether my ABN paperwork was in order.

The Dr+PC years, and a lot of driving

My first real contract came almost immediately — with Dr+PC. I was the onsite support person: house calls, business support calls, the whole “bring your laptop, I’ll be there in twenty minutes” experience. From there the work grew into something bigger than home visits. I ended up supporting multi-national contractors servicing national fast food chains and retail outlets, working on point-of-sale systems and EFTPOS terminals — the unglamorous but absolutely critical infrastructure that keeps a checkout running.

During the Townsville Computer Man years specifically, a lot of my time was spent on the road between Townsville, Ingham and Ayr, completing support calls. If you were a business owner in one of those towns with a dead POS terminal in 2010-something, there’s a fair chance I met you.

2016: a pause, not an ending

I paused the business in 2016. Not closed — paused. Life, other ventures and other priorities took over, and the name went quiet for a while. But “paused” is the operative word, and it’s why the name has stuck around ready for exactly this moment.

Why relaunch now

Today’s relaunch is deliberately more focused: repairs, done properly, for people who want experienced support rather than a call centre script. It’s part of a broader legacy I care about in Townsville’s IT space — a space that’s lost some important names along the way. The loss of Digital Matrix Computers left a real gap in the local market, and Townsville is now largely relying on interstate imports for same-day parts, sometimes facing up to seven days of postage delays during the wet season. That’s not good enough for a regional centre this size, and I’ve written before about why Townsville needs a large specialist computer store — I’d still love to see Computer Alliance or Umart take a serious look at setting up here.

Until that happens, Townsville Computer Man is here to fill the gap it always filled: reliable, experienced, local support — the kind that comes from someone who’s been doing this since before some of you had a Facebook account.

Welcome back

If you were a past customer, from the Dr+PC days, the POS and EFTPOS contract work, or the original support runs through Townsville, Ingham and Ayr — welcome back. If you’re new, welcome in. Either way, the name’s the same, the experience is a lot deeper than it was in 2009, and the service is available if you give us a call.

Check out the relaunched site at www.tsvcomputerman.com.au.
