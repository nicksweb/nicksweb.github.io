In August, I [looked at cyber security jobs advertised across North Queensland during 2025 and 2026]({% post_url 2026-08-14-north-queensland-cyber-security-jobs-2025-2026 %}). The market I found was small, but there were real opportunities in security operations, governance, identity and infrastructure.

This follow-up has a practical purpose: to highlight two recent Townsville advertisements, explain where they fit into a cyber security career, and consider what would make it easier to keep building that career locally.

Townsville City Council is advertising a dedicated Cyber Security Operations Officer. Townsville Catholic Education has also advertised an IT Service Delivery Specialist, a broader technology role that illustrates how people can develop the experience security work draws on. Together, they show why it is worth looking at both specialist security positions and the IT roles around them.

**Vacancy details were checked on 2 October 2026.** Council's advertised deadline is 14 October. The education role closes on 2 October itself, so I include it as a recent example rather than an opportunity readers should assume is still open.

## Council's role offers dedicated security operations work

The [Council advertisement](https://jobs.townsville.qld.gov.au/page.php?pageID=160&windowUID=0&AdvertID=930510) describes a hands-on position investigating security alerts and responding to incidents across its technology environment. The officer works with Council's Cyber Security Engineer and external managed security providers, acting as the first internal escalation point for incidents.

The work spans several connected areas:

- **Detection and investigation:** reviewing alerts and logs from security information and event management (SIEM), endpoint, identity, network and cloud platforms.
- **Incident response:** assessing threats, deciding what needs attention and documenting findings and recommendations.
- **Preventive work:** supporting threat hunting, tracking vulnerabilities and coordinating remediation with technology teams.
- **Ongoing improvement:** helping with audits, security controls, staff advice and awareness.

That breadth is what interests me. Someone in this position would need to connect a suspicious event with the systems, people and services it might affect, then help work out an appropriate response. It is a useful example of operational cyber security being performed within a regional organisation.

### Who is it suited to?

Council asks for **at least five years in IT, including three years in cyber or information security**, along with relevant tertiary qualifications. It also seeks experience with ISO 27001 and the ACSC Essential Eight; the listed professional certifications are desirable.

Those requirements make this an experienced practitioner role. Someone coming from systems administration, networking, cloud, endpoint management or an IT service provider could have relevant experience, particularly if security has already formed a substantial part of their work. General IT experience alone should not be mistaken for meeting the security requirement.

For candidates, the useful question is what evidence they can offer: incidents investigated, vulnerabilities resolved, controls improved and risks explained to people outside the IT team.

### Pay, conditions and applying

The LGO6 salary range is **$102,640–$109,563 plus superannuation**, for 36.25 hours a week and a nine-day fortnight. Advertised benefits include five weeks' annual leave, 17.5% leave loading and professional development. Council's advertised 13.5% employer super contribution is conditional on the employee contributing at least 6% of ordinary time earnings.

Those details matter when comparing the whole package. A sustainable regional career needs worthwhile work, reasonable conditions and room to develop.

Applications require a résumé and a cover letter of no more than two pages. **[Read the position description and apply through Council's vacancy page](https://jobs.townsville.qld.gov.au/page.php?pageID=160&windowUID=0&AdvertID=930510) before 11:45 pm on Wednesday 14 October 2026.**

## The IT experience behind a security career

Townsville Catholic Education's [IT Service Delivery Specialist advertisement](https://tceo-portal.applynow.net.au/jobs/TCE5129-it-service-delivery-specialist) offers a different example. Based at its Townsville City office, the permanent role carries an advertised salary of **$89,525–$102,735 plus superannuation**, depending on experience, with a proposed start date of 19 October.

The work involves complex troubleshooting, maintaining systems, supporting performance and contributing to technology projects across its school network. The employer seeks established IT support and infrastructure experience, alongside sound judgement and the ability to communicate with different stakeholders.

This is a broader IT role, and the advertisement does not promise a subsequent move into cyber security. Its relevance is the foundation that operational experience can provide.

Consider an administrator investigating why a user cannot access a service. Understanding identity, permissions, device configuration and network behaviour helps solve that problem. The same knowledge becomes valuable when investigating whether unusual access is legitimate, a configuration mistake or a security incident.

One possible progression is:

> IT support → systems, networks or infrastructure → increasing security responsibilities → a dedicated security role.

That progression takes deliberate learning and opportunities to practise. It also depends on the employer providing supervision, useful project work and access to more experienced colleagues. A job title by itself cannot guarantee those things.

The TCE vacancy's published deadline is **11:59 pm AEST on Friday 2 October 2026**. Check the [original advertisement](https://tceo-portal.applynow.net.au/jobs/TCE5129-it-service-delivery-specialist) for its status; the [employer's vacancy board](https://tceo-external.applynow.net.au/) is the better place to find subsequent opportunities.

## National demand needs local pathways

Jobs and Skills Australia's [October 2025 analysis](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/news/cyber-security-skills-demand-labour-market-evolves) reported about 70,900 people employed nationally as Database and Systems Administrators and ICT Security Specialists in August 2025. It projected 14.2% employment growth for that group between May 2024 and May 2029, compared with 6.6% across all employment.

These are dated national figures for a group **broader than cyber security alone**. They provide context, rather than a forecast of Townsville vacancies or a guarantee that a new graduate will readily find work.

The local challenge has been recognised for years. The information technology section of the [Townsville Workforce Development Plan 2020–2025, page 42](https://jobsqueensland.qld.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/townsville-workforce-development-plan.pdf#page=43), reported graduates leaving the region in search of stable employment. It also identified Townsville ICT workers supporting global projects remotely for interstate organisations, and proposed stronger local graduate pathways.

That older plan cannot tell us how many people are leaving today. It does show why publicising local opportunities matters: people need to see how they can start, specialise and progress here.

## Could more government digital careers be based here?

I would like to see Queensland and Australian Government employers give more deliberate consideration to locating suitable digital and cyber security positions in major regional centres.

Queensland's [Digital Economy Strategy workforce priority](https://www.qld.gov.au/government/strategies-initiatives/strategies/digital-economy-strategy/digital-market/pipeline-of-talent-for-digital-careers) identifies people in remote and regional communities as part of the talent pool it wants to develop. It also sets a goal of public sector leadership in digital skills.

Yet the [2027 Digital Graduate Program](https://www.qld.gov.au/jobs/graduates/graduate-programs/programs/digital), which includes cyber security roles, lists Brisbane as its location. Its [application guidance](https://www.qld.gov.au/jobs/career/digital-careers/digital-jobs/queensland-government-digital-graduate-program/how-to-apply) explicitly says all positions are based in Brisbane. Applications for that intake have already closed.

A current [Principal Cyber Security Analyst advertisement in the Queensland Government Cyber Security Unit](https://smartjobs.qld.gov.au/jobs/QLD-703232-26) provides another example. The permanent AO7 role involves technical leadership in incident response, detection and threat hunting, lists Brisbane Inner City as its workplace and closes on 16 October 2026.

These examples do not establish that every government digital job is concentrated in Brisbane. They do raise a useful question: could some future graduate places and specialist positions be designed to work from Townsville or other regional centres?

There are practical considerations. Some functions need secure facilities, close supervision or teams working together on site. A regional graduate placement also needs a mentor and meaningful work; simply giving someone a laptop and a remote manager is not enough.

But where the work allows it, regional placements supported by experienced staff, shared projects and clear promotion pathways could offer a lasting benefit. They could give local graduates a first opportunity and experienced practitioners another career step. The same question is worth asking across the broader Australian Public Service, with the location assessed against the actual requirements of each role.

## What to take from these advertisements

For someone already working in IT, Council's vacancy is worth reading closely against the security experience they can demonstrate. For someone building that experience, broader infrastructure and service-delivery roles deserve attention too. Look beyond the title and ask about responsibilities, mentoring and the work you would actually do.

I will keep watching both kinds of advertisement. Townsville needs opportunities at several stages: a first technology job, a chance to take on security responsibilities, a specialist position and somewhere to progress after that.

The encouraging part of these examples is that useful pieces of that career path are available locally. The next step is giving more people a realistic way to connect them, and continue developing their skills in North Queensland.
