A month ago, I wrote about [the problem with being called Nick in Townsville](/posts/the-problem-with-being-called-nick-in-townsville/). Google had plenty to say about Nick Dametto. Establishing that there was another local Nick took a bit more work.

Then I tried my full name and discovered [I was competing with a brewery](/posts/nicholas-townsville-competing-with-a-brewery/).

There is now some progress to report. In my latest checks, **Nicholas Townsville puts the original article on the first page**, while **Nick Townsville brings it up on the second page**. Google's AI Overview also includes me among the possible matches for Nicholas Townsville.

Selwyn Nicholas Brewing is still above me in the screenshot. They retain the considerable advantage of being able to offer visitors a beer. But the IT bloke is becoming easier to find.

Some of the most interesting discoveries in the wider website statistics have very little to do with searching for my name.

## Nicholas on page one, Nick on page two

In the [12 September update](/posts/nicholas-townsville-competing-with-a-brewery/), I recorded this website appearing around fourth for *Nicholas Townsville*. The new screenshot shows the original August article directly below Selwyn Nicholas Brewing in the visible organic results.

That is an encouraging change from the earlier check. It is also interesting that Google has chosen the article about being called **Nick**, even though the search uses **Nicholas**.

For *Nick Townsville*, my latest check found the same article on the second page. There is still some distance to go, but it is a useful milestone for the search that started this experiment.

These are observations from my own searches. [Google explains that results vary with time, location, device and recent search history](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553). I am recording what I saw so there is something concrete to compare with later.

The Search Console export below does **not** contain rows for either exact query, *nick townsville* or *nicholas townsville*. It cannot give me a 28-day average position for those two phrases. The screenshots and my checks provide that part of the story.

## Google has also noticed the experiment

The expanded AI Overview for *Nicholas Townsville* includes **Nicholas O'Sullivan**, describes my connection with IT and cyber security in Townsville, and even mentions the personal SEO experiment.

![Expanded Google AI Overview for Nicholas Townsville including Nicholas O'Sullivan as a Townsville IT and cyber security professional and referring to the personal SEO experiment](/assets/images/AI-Results-Nicholas-Included.png)
*Google's expanded AI Overview, supplied for this September update. The entry about me includes this website among its sources.*

I find that slightly funny. I wrote about trying to help Google work out which Nick I was, and Google is now explaining that I am the Nick who wrote about trying to help Google work out which Nick I was.

My name, location and professional work are appearing together. That is the association I wanted somebody searching for me to find. Because the Overview cites this site, it is drawing on my own account of who I am; the useful result here is that people can discover it.

[Google's guidance on AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says the usual SEO foundations still apply, including useful content, internal links and structured data that matches the visible page. It also says AI Overview and AI Mode traffic is included in Search Console's **Web** reporting. This export does not isolate how many clicks, if any, came through that AI result.

## What the 28 days of numbers show

The Search Console export covers **28 August to 24 September 2026**, with the search type set to **Web**. Its page list includes both this website and the older `blog.nickosullivan.id.au` site, so the overall totals cover both.

Across those 28 days, the daily chart records **139 clicks** and **10,133 impressions**, giving a **1.37% click-through rate**. Impressions count appearances in search results; they are not visits or unique readers.

Breaking the period into four equal weeks makes the shape of the month much clearer:

| Week | Search impressions | Clicks to the site | Click-through rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 28 August–3 September | 783 | 20 | 2.55% |
| 4–10 September | 820 | 22 | 2.68% |
| 11–17 September | 7,518 | 73 | 0.97% |
| 18–24 September | 1,012 | 24 | 2.37% |

Comparing the two halves of the export, clicks rose from **42 to 97**, an increase of about **131%**, while impressions rose from **1,603 to 8,530**, about **432%**.

Those are large percentage changes from a small starting point. The weekly breakdown shows why they need some context: there was a substantial spike in the middle of September, followed by a quieter week.

The final week still recorded about **29% more impressions** and **20% more clicks** than the first. That is a modest, encouraging comparison, although four weeks is too short to declare a lasting growth trend.

## The stories people are finding

The biggest contributor was [my article remembering Townsville's Great Northern Hotel](/posts/great-northern-hotel-townsville-fire-memories/). Its page recorded **6,849 impressions and 55 clicks** over the reporting period. That is roughly **40% of all clicks** in the export.

The daily impression peak in the export is labelled **12 September**, with **2,679 impressions** across the property. The fire and the article's publication were on 11 September in Townsville. The timing and page totals point to a strong connection with interest in that story, although the export does not break each day's traffic down by page.

That article was a personal account of a familiar Townsville building and my walk to work. People finding it through searches about the hotel, its history and photographs is a reminder that local memories can be useful to somebody else.

Other writing is finding an audience too. The [Townsville gym data-breach article](/posts/nq-cyber-watch-townsville-gym-data-breach-police-officers/) recorded **386 impressions and 11 clicks**, while [my look back at Townsville computer stores](/posts/townsville-computer-stores-past-and-future/) recorded **347 impressions and 8 clicks**.

Even the older [Aussie Broadband IPv6 configuration article](https://blog.nickosullivan.id.au/2021/04/configuring-ipv6-with-aussie-broadband/) appeared **358 times** and received **3 clicks**. A practical technical note can still be found years after it was written.

## Helping people find the same person

For anyone arriving through one of these searches, I am **Nicholas “Nick” O'Sullivan**, a Townsville IT and cyber security professional. This website brings together my work, technical writing and observations about life in North Queensland. [My About page](/about/) is the fuller introduction.

I also own [nicholasosullivan.id.au](https://nicholasosullivan.id.au/), which redirects to this website at `www.nickosullivan.id.au`. It gives anyone who remembers the full version of my name another straightforward way to find me. Nick or Nicholas, both addresses lead to the same person and the same writing.

I have also updated [my About.me profile](https://about.me/nicholasosullivan). It is another place where someone can find me and connect the name with this website.

My YouTube channel, [TechnologyLowdown](https://www.youtube.com/TechnologyLowdown), is another part of that picture. I occasionally post technical videos there, sharing some of my interest in technology in a different format.

Keeping those introductions consistent remains part of the work. The About.me update is a recent addition; these figures cannot establish whether it has influenced any ranking. There have been several changes and new articles during the month, so I cannot isolate one edit as the cause of the progress.

The two earlier SEO stories are being found in their own right: the August article recorded **57 impressions and 5 clicks**, and the brewery follow-up recorded **126 impressions and 3 clicks**. Those page totals include all the searches that brought them into view, not just the two Townsville name searches.

## Where the experiment goes next

The next useful check is whether the name searches keep showing this site, whether *Nick Townsville* reaches the first page in my checks, and whether relevant articles continue attracting readers after the hotel-related spike has passed.

I will compare another full reporting period, watch the more specific versions of my name, and keep writing about the Townsville technology and community subjects I can contribute to. Someone finding an answer in an old technical note is every bit as useful an outcome as someone finding my About page.

The brewery is still ahead in the screenshot. My occasional Great Northern Super Crisp remains an occasional Great Northern Super Crisp. But there is finally a little more evidence that Google has room for this Townsville Nicholas too.
