An IT consultant can help when your organisation faces a technology decision that needs more investigation than the usual support request. You may have a quote you cannot confidently assess, an upgrade that affects several systems, or competing priorities for a limited budget.

For a Townsville business, school, charity or community organisation, the useful outcome is a decision you understand and a practical plan for acting on it. This is the kind of work I offer through my [IT consulting service](/consulting/).

## Decisions that benefit from a review

There are several common reasons to seek advice:

- **A connection upgrade.** You are considering FTTN-to-FTTP and need to understand the implications for the firewall, phones, cabling and service transition.
- **An ageing network.** Wi-Fi or links between buildings are limiting work, and you need to decide which parts to keep or replace.
- **A substantial quote.** You want the requirements, alternatives and ongoing costs explained before committing.
- **A firewall migration.** You need a plan for preparation, interruption, service checks and recovery.
- **An unclear Microsoft 365 environment.** Ownership, administrator access, security settings or backup responsibilities need attention.
- **A budget or growth decision.** You need to separate immediate work from improvements that can wait.

A review can also help when everyone agrees there is a problem but each supplier sees only one part of it.

## How consulting fits with your existing provider

Routine support keeps people working: handling faults, maintaining systems and responding to day-to-day requests. Consulting focuses on an agreed question, decision or project. An existing provider may offer both, and an additional perspective can help check assumptions or clarify a difficult brief.

You do not need to replace a provider to seek a second opinion. Responsibilities should be explicit, especially where a recommendation affects systems someone else supports. The engagement should make collaboration easier by documenting the requirements and decisions.

## What to prepare

Start with a short explanation of the problem, who it affects and what a successful outcome would look like. Bring any relevant quotes, diagrams, equipment lists, service contracts or previous findings that you already have.

Include timing constraints and an indicative budget. A change that must happen during a school holiday or before an office move needs a different plan from a gradual improvement programme. You do not need perfect documentation to start; identifying the gaps can be part of the scope.

## What a useful engagement should produce

Agree the deliverables before work begins. Depending on the brief, these could be a review of a proposal, an options comparison, prioritised recommendations, a network design or a migration plan.

Recommendations should explain why the proposed solution fits. If an Aruba platform costs more than a Ubiquiti alternative, the comparison should identify the capabilities, support and operating requirements that matter to your organisation. If the less expensive option meets the brief, that should be clear too. Compare the complete proposed solutions, including the work and continuing costs needed to operate them.
