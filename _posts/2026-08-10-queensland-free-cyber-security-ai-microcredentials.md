I keep coming back to the same theme on this blog: most of the risk small businesses, schools and not-for-profits carry isn't a technology gap, it's a literacy gap. Boards, owners and managers making reasonable-sounding decisions without quite the right frame of reference. So when a government program shows up offering to close that gap for free, it's worth a proper look rather than a skim.

Two separate Queensland Government-funded programs currently qualify.

## The $11.5 million UniSC micro-credentials program

On 29 July 2026, the Crisafulli Government announced an expanded $11.5 million investment in free micro-credential courses for small and family businesses, delivered by the University of the Sunshine Coast. It's part of the government's [Small and Family Business First Action Statement](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/105704){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} — an election commitment rather than a pilot program — and six new courses are being added by 2029.

Three courses are open for enrolment now, all free for Queensland-based small and family businesses:

- **Business Planning** — 2 hours a week for 6 weeks
- **Continuity Planning** — building resilience into how the organisation operates
- **Workforce Planning for Business** — a 7-week course on productivity and operational efficiency

Registrations for 2026 intakes are opening soon through [UniSC's business planning course page](https://www.unisc.edu.au/study/courses-and-programs/short-courses-and-microcredentials/queensland-government-funded-microcredential-courses/business-planning){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}.

The courses I'd actually flag for this audience — **artificial intelligence, cyber security and procurement** — are still in development, expected early 2027. [Register interest now](https://www.unisc.edu.au/businessmicro#EOI){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} if any of those are relevant to you; it's currently the only way to get notified the moment enrolments open.

## A free cyber security micro-credential you can do sooner

Separately from the UniSC program, TAFE Queensland already runs a free, stand-alone cyber security micro-credential through the [Queensland Future Skills Partnership](https://tafeqld.edu.au/campaigns/queensland-future-skills-partnership/micro-credentials){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} — led by BHP Mitsubishi Alliance, with TAFE Queensland and CQUniversity as education partners, funded through investment from the Queensland Resources Industry Development Plan.

It's short — roughly 6 to 8 hours, blended across a self-paced online module, a practical activity and a facilitated webinar or workshop — and it's aimed at exactly the audience I'd want reading this: people who need working awareness of cyber security risk without becoming a technical specialist. The course covers:

- Current and emerging trends in cyber security
- Cyber security threats and their business impact
- The legislation and frameworks relevant to cyber security in Australia
- How to support cyber security efforts within a workplace
- How current technology may reshape cyber security practice going forward

Expressions of interest are particularly encouraged from workers in regional locations, which is most of Queensland outside the south-east corner.

## Who this is actually for

Neither program requires a technical background, and that's the point. I wrote recently about [ASD's frontier AI guidance for boards]({% post_url 2026-08-06-asd-frontier-ai-guidance-for-boards %}), and the same argument applies here: cyber security literacy at board and ownership level isn't a nice-to-have anymore, it's a governance responsibility, and it doesn't require anyone to become an engineer to meet it. A short, free, government-funded course is about as low-friction a way as exists to build that literacy — the TAFE cyber security micro-credential in particular is a sensible fit for a business owner, manager or board member who wants a working grasp of the landscape rather than a technical qualification.

What free training won't do is tell you whether your own organisation's email, accounts and network are actually configured properly today — that's a different question, and one Suburban Secure works through directly via [managed IT services](https://suburbansecure.au/managed-it-services/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} and [network security services](https://suburbansecure.au/network-security-services/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}. But building the literacy first tends to make that conversation a much better one.
