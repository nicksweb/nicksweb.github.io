Cyber security in Cairns: FNQ businesses, incidents, training and help

Cyber security in Cairns: FNQ businesses, incidents, training and help

Cairns has a cyber security story that reaches well beyond tourism. Cairns Regional Council’s economic overview describes a service economy spanning health, education, accommodation, retail and transport. Each of those sectors depends on information moving between people and organisations, often through systems the local business does not operate itself.

I work in IT in Townsville, and write about what cyber security means across regional Queensland. For Cairns, the useful starting point is the information trail: who collects it, who receives a copy, and who can explain what happens when a supplier has an incident?

The Westco Motors claim, and the limits of the evidence

In my August 2026 Cyber Watch report on Westco Motors Cairns, I covered reporting that the Storm ransomware group had named the dealership and published sample documents. The article records an allegation, not a completed forensic finding or confirmation from the dealership. That evidence status belongs with the story whenever it is summarised.

A dealership is a useful example of a complicated information trail: sales, servicing, parts, finance and supplier relationships overlap. That is analysis of the business model, not a claim that every kind of information was involved in the alleged incident. The incident tracker retains the source and the date of the account.

The practical question for another Cairns business is where its own customer documents end up. A booking platform, shared mailbox, finance application and exported spreadsheet can all hold different copies of the same person’s details. Knowing which copy is authoritative, who can access it and when it should be removed takes more work than checking that a laptop has antivirus.

Tourism and service businesses: follow the handover

For a tour operator, accommodation provider or local service business, I would start by walking through one customer transaction. These are review prompts, not findings about any named business:

  1. Which booking, payment and customer communication systems are involved?
  2. Does each staff member have their own account, and who removes access when a casual worker or contractor leaves?
  3. Can a supplier access the environment without someone at the business knowing?
  4. How would staff confirm a booking or contact a customer if the main platform were unavailable?
  5. Who checks a request to change bank details, using contact information already held by the business?

That last handover matters because accurate customer details can make a fraudulent message convincing. The national Nick Scali incident covered in Cyber Watch also shows why a retailer’s central systems can matter to customers in Cairns without there being a separate attack on a Cairns store.

Training infrastructure and pathways

TAFE Queensland’s Cairns campus describes a cyber security training facility completed in 2023 for introductory and advanced IT security and networking courses. That is a tangible regional asset. It doesn’t establish that a particular qualification has a bookable intake today; ask for the course, delivery mode, dates and prerequisites.

JCU’s Bachelor of Information Technology lists Cairns among its study locations and cybersecurity among its subject areas. Check the actual course and campus rather than assuming every degree with a university’s name is offered locally.

My North Queensland jobs research provides another part of the picture. It records advertised roles across a defined period, rather than promising that a qualification leads directly to a dedicated security job in Cairns. Broader infrastructure, support and identity roles can carry substantial security responsibility too.
