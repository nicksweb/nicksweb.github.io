## LinkedIn draft

I've brought my regional cyber security writing together in one place.

There are guides for Townsville, Cairns, Mackay and Rockhampton, alongside a tracker of the incidents I've covered in North Queensland Cyber Watch.

The tracker needs a qualification up front: it is a sourced index of my reporting, not a count of every attack in regional Queensland. A company-confirmed incident, a newspaper report and a ransomware group's claim are different kinds of evidence. I've kept that distinction beside each entry.

The regional guides also ask different questions. Cairns has customer information moving through tourism and service businesses. Mackay has industrial dependencies where an IT outage can quickly become somebody else's operational problem. Rockhampton raises questions about regional services and building local capability. Townsville already has people, training and employers; the question is how those pieces connect.

If you know of a useful public source I've missed, I'd welcome it.

## YouTube outline and description

Working title: What regional Queensland cyber incidents can actually tell us

Opening: “A ransomware headline can tell you that somebody has made a claim. It doesn't necessarily tell you what happened. I've been collecting regional cyber stories, and that distinction follows you through almost every one.”

Show the tracker, then explain one confirmed incident, one allegation and one national platform with regional connections. Use the Mackay Sugar company notice and the dated Westco article as contrasting evidence examples; recheck both before recording. Do not display stolen documents, personal information or criminal leak sites.

Show the date-basis and evidence columns. Explain why eight entries cannot establish a regional attack rate. Finish with three questions: where is our information, who can reach it, and what would we do if the system were unavailable?
