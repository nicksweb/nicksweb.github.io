# Working article concept

## Possible headline

**More cyber security opportunities are appearing in Townsville — but there is room for more**

Alternative:

**Building a cyber security career in Townsville is becoming more realistic**

Alternative:

**Two more technology opportunities show why Townsville's cyber workforce matters**

## Central argument

My previous look at cyber security employment across North Queensland showed a relatively small but genuine regional market.

There have not suddenly been dozens of new cyber security positions since then, but several recent vacancies are worth highlighting because they demonstrate something important: people wanting to build technology and cyber security careers in Townsville do have pathways available without automatically moving to Brisbane, Sydney, Melbourne or Canberra.

Townsville City Council's current Cyber Security Operations Officer vacancy is particularly interesting because it is a dedicated operational security position dealing with SIEM, endpoint, identity, network and cloud telemetry, incident response, threat hunting and vulnerability management.

At the same time, broader infrastructure and service-delivery positions such as Townsville Catholic Education's IT Service Delivery Specialist remain an important part of the regional career pipeline. Many cyber security professionals do not begin in a job carrying "cyber security" in its title. They build their knowledge through service desk, systems administration, networking, cloud, identity and infrastructure roles before progressively specialising.

The encouraging part is seeing both types of opportunity appearing locally.

The unresolved question is how much further this could go — particularly if Queensland and Australian Government agencies deliberately established more digital and cyber security roles in major regional centres such as Townsville.

# 2. Townsville City Council: Cyber Security Operations Officer

This should be the centrepiece of the article.

## Why it stands out

This is not simply a general IT position where "cyber security" appears somewhere in the selection criteria.

It is a dedicated operational security role.

The responsibilities include:

- SIEM monitoring and investigation
- endpoint security
- identity security
- network and cloud security monitoring
- incident triage and response
- working with managed security service providers
- threat hunting
- vulnerability management
- technical incident reporting
- auditing and control assessments
- security-control improvement
- cyber security support and awareness.

That combination is valuable because it gives someone exposure to many of the functions normally associated with a Security Operations Centre or internal blue-team/security-operations function.

## Important wording point

I would NOT describe this literally as an "entry-level cyber security job".

Council asks for:

- five years of IT experience; and
- three years in cyber or information security.

That clearly puts it above graduate or true entry level.

Instead describe it as something like:

> one of the better opportunities currently available in Townsville for someone moving further into a dedicated cyber security career.

Or:

> an unusually accessible operational cyber security opportunity in a regional market where many advertised security positions tend to require senior engineering, architecture, consulting or management experience.

It may particularly suit somebody who has already spent several years in:

- systems administration
- networking
- infrastructure
- security-oriented IT support
- endpoint administration
- Microsoft/cloud administration
- MSP environments

and wants their next role to be primarily cyber security.

### Role summary

Townsville City Council is seeking a Cyber Security Operations Officer to join its Cyber Security team.

The hands-on operational position focuses on monitoring, investigating and responding to security alerts and events across Council's technology environment.

The officer works closely with managed security service providers and Council's Cyber Security Engineer and acts as the first internal escalation point for cyber security incidents.

The position also contributes to threat hunting, vulnerability management, cyber hygiene, security auditing and continual improvement of Council's cyber security posture.

### Responsibilities captured from advertisement

- Monitor, analyse and investigate cyber security alerts, events and incidents across SIEM, endpoint protection, identity, network and cloud security platforms.
- Act as the primary internal escalation point for security alerts and incidents raised by Council's managed security service providers.
- Identify, assess, triage and respond to threats and incidents under Council's incident-response processes.
- Review logs and security telemetry from multiple sources.
- Prepare technical incident reports and recommendations.
- Support threat hunting, security monitoring and incident investigations with the Cyber Security Engineer.
- Coordinate vulnerability identification, remediation tracking and reporting with technology teams.
- Assist with security reviews, audits and control assessments.
- Identify cyber security risks, compliance gaps and opportunities for improvement.
- Assist with implementing, monitoring and improving security controls and operational processes.
- Provide first-level cyber security support and advice.
- Contribute to awareness initiatives and promotion of security best practice.

### Candidate requirements captured from advertisement

- Relevant tertiary qualifications in Cyber Security, Information Systems or similar.
- Postgraduate study highly regarded.
- At least five years' IT experience.
- At least three years' cyber or information-security experience.
- Certifications such as CISM, CISA, GIAC GSEC or OSCP desirable.
- Experience with ISO 27001 and the ACSC Essential Eight.
- Knowledge of cyber security principles, security controls and risk management.
- Incident-investigation capability.
- Ability to assess risk and communicate business impact and treatment options.
- Strong stakeholder communication.
- Analytical and problem-solving skills.

### Article observations

This is effectively a broad blue-team/security-operations role rather than a narrowly specialised position.

Someone doing this job could gain practical exposure across detection, incident response, vulnerability management, threat hunting, governance/control assessment and stakeholder communication.

That breadth can be particularly valuable in regional organisations where security teams are generally smaller and practitioners often work across multiple disciplines.

# 4. Technology careers don't always start with "cyber" in the job title

Use Townsville Catholic Education as the second example.

This lets the article avoid giving people the impression that the only pathway into cyber security is:

university → Cyber Security Analyst.

A more common progression can be:

IT support → systems/infrastructure/networking → security responsibilities → dedicated security role.

The TCE vacancy isn't a cyber security vacancy, but it demonstrates the sort of locally available technology position from which people can build experience in complex organisational IT environments.

The role involves troubleshooting, infrastructure, systems performance, service delivery and project work across a geographically distributed education environment.

Those foundational skills remain highly relevant to cyber security.

It is difficult to secure systems properly without understanding how they actually operate.

# 7. Put the local opportunities into national context

Useful statistic:

Jobs and Skills Australia estimated approximately **70,900 people** were employed nationally as Database and Systems Administrators and ICT Security Specialists in August 2025.

That represented growth of about **3,300 workers over the preceding year**.

Employment in the occupational group was projected to increase **14.2% between May 2024 and May 2029**, compared with **6.6% for employment overall**.

Its major employing industries include:

- Professional, Scientific and Technical Services
- Public Administration and Safety
- Financial and Insurance Services.

### Why it matters locally

This is particularly relevant to Townsville because Public Administration and Safety is already an important part of the local economy.

Townsville City Council's current economic profile reports approximately:

- **206,260 population**
- **104,832 local jobs**
- **108,331 employed residents**
- **$16.02 billion GRP**

and identifies **Public Administration and Safety as Townsville's largest industry**.

That creates a sensible argument that cyber capability is not something relevant only to technology companies.

Townsville's major institutions — government, Defence, health, education, utilities, resources and other large organisations — all operate increasingly complex technology environments requiring cyber security capability.

Regional Australia continued to record lower vacancy-fill rates than metropolitan Australia, and the gap widened to **4.6 percentage points**.

# 9. The missing piece: more government digital careers in regional Queensland

This could become the strongest closing section.

Something along these lines:

It is encouraging seeing Townsville City Council developing cyber security capability locally.

What I would particularly like to see next is more state and federal government digital and cyber security employment deliberately located outside South East Queensland.

There is an interesting contrast in Queensland Government's own workforce strategy.

Its Digital Economy Strategy identifies people in **remote and regional communities** as one of the cohorts from which Queensland can increase its digital talent pool.

Its goals include developing a highly skilled digital workforce and positioning the Queensland public sector as a leader in digital skills.

Yet the Queensland Government's **2027 Digital Graduate Program**, which includes cyber security among its career streams, states that **all positions are based in Brisbane**.

That is a useful fact, rather than merely an opinion about centralisation.

### Possible wording

There is nothing inherently wrong with Brisbane hosting large central cyber security teams. Whole-of-government functions need concentration, specialist capability and strong collaboration.

But not every digital role necessarily needs to be located there.

Regional positions could:

- provide pathways for regional graduates
- retain experienced technology workers
- build local professional communities
- increase regional cyber capability
- provide another career step between local IT roles and senior capital-city positions
- expose government organisations to staff with first-hand understanding of regional environments.

The role covers:

- incident detection and response
- threat intelligence
- analytics
- threat hunting
- whole-of-government cyber defence.

The advertised workplace location is **Brisbane Inner City**.

Similarly, the 2027 Queensland Government Digital Graduate Program includes roles in:

- cyber security
- cloud/digital engineering
- networking
- automation
- software
- data
- artificial intelligence

but its published eligibility information says:

> All positions are based in Brisbane.

This gives the article a solid basis for asking:

**Could at least some future Queensland Government digital and cyber positions be based in major regional centres?**

That is probably a stronger formulation than arguing that government "should move jobs out of Brisbane". It invites discussion about workforce development.

# 11. Federal government angle

Be careful not to overstate this.

ASD naturally has particular geographic and security requirements.

Its entry-level information says many programs are based in Canberra and relocation is often involved.

At the same time, ASD describes cyber careers spanning:

- ICT Security Specialist
- Information Security Analyst
- Cyber Security Advice and Assessment
- Cyber Threat Analyst
- Malware Analyst
- Operations Coordinator

among other functions.

The observation could therefore be:

National-security agencies understandably have location and security constraints that differ from ordinary employers. Nevertheless, across the broader Australian Public Service there may be opportunities to consider whether more digital and cyber work can be performed from regional centres.

Avoid making ASD itself the primary target of the argument.

# 12. Historical Townsville workforce evidence

There is actually an excellent older source that reinforces this argument.

The **Townsville Workforce Development Plan 2020–2025** identified concerns that Townsville graduates were leaving the region seeking stable employment.

It argued that promoting locally available opportunities could help retain the region's future workforce.

It also identified examples of ICT professionals living in Townsville while working remotely on national and global projects.

That gives this article continuity:

Regional technology workforce retention is not a newly invented problem.

Townsville has been discussing it for years.

The appearance of additional cyber and technology roles locally is therefore worth recognising.

---

# 13. Positive conclusion

Avoid finishing negatively.

Possible direction:

The Townsville technology market is not suddenly overflowing with cyber security jobs.

But that probably isn't the right benchmark.

What matters is whether there are enough opportunities for someone to start in technology locally, build experience, specialise and continue progressing without reaching a point where moving south becomes the only realistic career option.

Positions such as Townsville Catholic Education's Service Delivery Specialist provide part of that foundation.

A dedicated position such as Townsville City Council's Cyber Security Operations Officer provides another step in the pathway.

Every additional organisation building that capability locally makes the regional technology ecosystem a little stronger.

And if state and federal governments increasingly looked at North Queensland not simply as somewhere requiring digital services, but somewhere from which those services and cyber capabilities could also be delivered, that pathway could become considerably stronger.

---

# Potential final paragraph

Cyber security is ultimately a national capability, but the workforce does not have to exist entirely in capital cities. Townsville has universities, Defence, local government, health services, education providers, utilities, resources companies, managed service providers and a sizeable professional workforce. Seeing local organisations invest in dedicated security capability is encouraging. The next opportunity is making sure people who develop those skills in North Queensland can continue developing their careers here.
