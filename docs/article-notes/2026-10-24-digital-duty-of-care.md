## Source and status checks

- The user supplied a friend's LinkedIn post and the department's 20 October announcement. The draft acknowledges the prompt without quoting the post or inventing the friend's identity.
- Department overview and Minister's release identify introduction as 15 October 2026. The linked news page is dated 20 October. Those dates describe different events.
- The Australian Human Rights Commission submission concerns the September exposure draft. The Minister's 15 October release says the introduced version clarifies exemptions for professional news and entertainment media and parliamentary scrutiny of additional harms. The article distinguishes these sources and attributes the announced changes.
- Parliament's bill register could not be retrieved during research or the pre-publication recheck (403/inaccessible). No claim to have read or compared the introduced bill's clauses is made. Legislative description relies on the linked government summaries. The department's overview was rechecked on 24 October and continues to describe the reforms as proposed and the penalties as conditional on passage. The article retains that qualified description. Parliamentary reference for future updates: https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553
- The $109.2 million figure is the government's stated maximum; it is not presented as an automatic penalty, a fine per incident or a penalty applying to schools merely because they purchase software.
- The potential for over-restriction is framed as the author's concern, not an established consequence or a claim that all earlier objections remain unchanged in the introduced version.

## Personal experience and examples

- The reference to Queensland state schools, independent-school IT, infrastructure, cloud platforms, device management and vendor coordination comes from the existing `_tabs/about.md`.
- The app review and monitoring-alert examples are explicitly hypothetical. They do not describe incidents at the author's employer or make claims about particular students, staff or products.
- The user subsequently supplied experience of consulting application risk review registers in state schools and considering filtering/monitoring tools that make information available to wellbeing teams. These experiences are incorporated without naming schools, claiming a particular vendor was deployed, or inventing an incident or outcome.
- The user suggested ST4S in the independent-school context. Its public documentation confirms wider participation across government, Catholic and independent sectors and New Zealand. The article explains its assessment and catalogue roles without implying automatic approval for every use or universal public access to reports.
- The technical explanation distinguishes DNS domain filtering from search-term capture: a DNS-only log does not contain Google search queries. The article avoids an unnecessary OSI-layer reference and does not imply all filtering products or deployments have the same visibility.
- Linewize's public documentation is cited as an example of supported search-term alerts and recipient scoping, not as a procurement recommendation or a claim about a tool the author has used. No vendor effectiveness or accuracy claims are adopted.
- School recommendations are the author's proposed practice, not a claim that the bill mandates a particular product, checklist or monitoring arrangement.
